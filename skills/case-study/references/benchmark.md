# Why the v2 format looks like this

Comparison run on 2026-09-11. Our seven published outcomes (ManagingLife, iCardio.ai,
inPowered, Bridj, Huginmunin, Horizon Cloud, Aster) against Cloudelligent
(Purity Health), SquareOps (OurShopee) and CloudRaft.

| | CloudDrove today | Cloudelligent | SquareOps | CloudRaft |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own URL per case study | no, modal on /outcomes | yes | yes | yes |
| Numbers in headline or results | no, "Lower", "Tested" | yes: 70%, 85%, $50k | yes, some are literal bracketed placeholders | yes, short and named |
| Client quote inside the page | no, quotes live separately | no | no | yes |
| Cloud services listed (Partner Central needs this) | partial, tags only | yes, dedicated section | no | no |
| Engagement model and duration stated | no | no | yes | no |
| Narrative quality (situation, before/after, outcome) | yes, best of the four | thin | list-heavy | short |
| Key takeaways, related study, CTA | no | yes | yes | yes |
| OG image, reading time, share | no | yes | yes | yes |

## Verdict

Our writing is already the strongest of the four. Our packaging is the weakest, and
packaging is what a buyer sees first. Keep the narrative, fix the container.

Two things nobody else in the comparison does, so they are the differentiators worth
protecting:

1. A client quote inside the study (only CloudRaft has it, and theirs are short).
2. The agents line. No competitor can write "an agent drafted the findings and a
   named engineer signed off", because none of them have the agents or the 200
   open-source repos behind them.
